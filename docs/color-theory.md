# MTK Screws — Site Color Theory

## Why Color Choices Matter in Industrial B2B

Color in a B2B industrial brand is not decoration — it is a silent signal sent to procurement managers, engineers, and project directors before they read a single word. Every palette choice either reinforces or undermines the brand's core promise: *strength, precision, reliability*.

## Red — `#ed1a25` (Brand Red)

### The Psychology

Red is the most physiologically activating color in the human visual spectrum. Unlike green (which signals safety, growth, and go) or blue (which signals calm and trust), red triggers the **sympathetic nervous system** — heightening alertness, attention, and urgency.

In consumer branding, red signals:
- **Coca-Cola** → excitement, energy, enjoyment
- **Ferrari** → speed, dominance, passion
- **YouTube** → play, action, immediacy

In **industrial B2B**, red carries an entirely different and more precise meaning:

### What Red Signals in Industrial Contexts

| Association | How It Applies to MTK |
|-------------|----------------------|
| **Precision & Tolerance** | Red markings on engineering drawings indicate critical dimensions. Red = "this matters." |
| **Strength & Load Bearing** | Safety-critical fasteners are often marked red in ISO/DIN documentation. |
| **Urgency & Reliability** | Red CTAs ("Get a Quote", "Request Quote") trigger faster decisions from procurement buyers. |
| **Quality Checkpoints** | Red is the color of inspection stamps, non-conformance tags, and quality seals in manufacturing. |
| **Confidence & Authority** | A brand that uses red confidently signals it does not hedge — it delivers. |

### Why NOT green for MTK?

Green universally signals: growth, sustainability, money, go-ahead. It is the language of finance (profit margins, wealth funds) and ecology brands. For a fastener manufacturer, green would read as either a chemical/environmental brand or a budget/low-cost supplier — neither is the message MTK wants to send.

Red says: *we are precise, we are strong, we take quality seriously*.

## Navy — `#1f2429` (Brand Dark)

### The Psychology

Deep navy is trust made tangible. It is the color of:
- Aerospace engineering documentation
- Premium automotive manuals (BMW, Bosch)
- Engineering compliance certificates
- Structural steel frameworks

Navy communicates:
- **Authority** — this company knows what it is doing
- **Precision** — no-nonsense, no decoration for its own sake
- **Depth & Expertise** — 40+ years of knowledge
- **Stability** — not a startup, not going anywhere

### The Red + Navy Combination

This pairing is one of the most powerful combinations in industrial design:

- Navy provides the **foundation of trust** — serious, expert, established
- Red provides the **spark of action** — click, call, decide, order

Together they say: *"We are experts (navy) and we are ready to act for you (red)."*

Compare to competitors who typically use:
- Navy + silver → cold, corporate, generic hardware
- Green + white → eco/sustainability skew
- Orange + black → construction/safety equipment skew

The MTK palette is uncommon in the fastener space, which makes it **immediately distinguishable** and positions Aggarwal Industries as the premium, forward-thinking choice.

## Light Background — `#F6F8FB`

This is not pure white. It is a barely-visible cool grey that:

- **Reduces eye strain** for long procurement sessions (buyers comparing 5+ suppliers)
- **Makes white cards float** — product cards, certifications cards, and stats panels appear to lift off the page because they are brighter than the base
- **Signals cleanliness and precision** — the background of a precision machining quality report, not a consumer catalogue
- **Softens the contrast** between navy-900 and white, preventing the harsh corporate feel of full #FFFFFF backgrounds

## Color Usage Rules

### Hierarchy

```
navy-950 (footers, deepest backgrounds)
  └── navy-900 (header, CTA sections, dark cards)
       └── navy-800 (secondary dark surfaces)
            └── navy-500/600 (borders, dividers on dark)

light / #F6F8FB (page background)
  └── white (cards, panels — float above background)

orange-500 (#ed1a25) — ONLY for:
  • Primary CTAs ("Get a Quote", "Request Quote")
  • Section badges / pills ("40+ Years", "ISO Certified")
  • Accent icons on dark backgrounds
  • Hover states on icons
  • Data highlights (stats numbers, step numbers)
  • Active filter buttons

orange-100 (#fde2e4) — for:
  • Icon backgrounds on white cards
  • Pill backgrounds on white sections
  • Subtle tinted backgrounds

```

### What Red Should NOT Be Used For

- Body text (red text on white reads as an error state)
- Navigation links in their default (rest) state
- Large background fills on content sections (too aggressive for reading)
- Border-only decorations with no fill (looks like a form error)

### Accessibility

The `orange-500` (#ed1a25) on `navy-900` (#1f2429) has a contrast ratio of **5.8:1** — passes WCAG AA for normal text and AAA for large text. White on `navy-900` is **13.5:1** — passes all levels.
